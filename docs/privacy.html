Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 21, 2026

OpenReader ("the app") is a document reader for Android, Windows, macOS and Linux. This policy explains what data the app handles, what leaves your device and what never does.

In short

  • Your documents and their contents are read on your device and are never uploaded.
  • There are no accounts, no sign-in and no advertising.
  • The mobile app sends crash reports and usage analytics to Google Firebase. These do not include your documents, their names or their contents.
  • The app checks for updates (Google Play on Android, GitHub on desktop).

What stays on your device

To provide features like Browse, Search, History and Favorites, OpenReader keeps a local record of documents you open (file name, location, size, type and dates), your reading history, reading positions, favorites, page previews and your settings. This is stored in an on-device database, local app preferences and the app's cache. It is not sent to the developer or to any third party. You can clear reading history and the cache in Settings, and choose how long history is kept.

Reading text in scanned pages (OCR)

When a PDF page is a picture (a scan), OpenReader can read the text in it so you can select and copy it. This happens on your device, using text recognition built into your system or bundled with the app: Google ML Kit's on-device model on Android, Apple Vision on macOS and iOS, Windows' built-in OCR on Windows, and the Tesseract program on Linux when it is installed. The page image and the recognised text are not uploaded, are not sent to Firebase or any third party, and are not logged. The temporary image used for recognition is deleted as soon as the text has been read, and the recognised text is kept only in memory while the document is open.

Crash reports and usage analytics (Android)

Release builds of the Android app use two Google Firebase services. They are not used in the desktop apps.

  • Firebase Crashlytics sends a report when the app crashes or hits a serious error: the error and where in the app's code it happened, the app version, device model, operating system version, and a random identifier for this installation.
  • Google Analytics for Firebase records how the app is used, such as app opens, sessions and screens viewed, together with the app version, device model, operating system, language, an app-instance identifier and an approximate location (country or region) that Google derives from your network address. It may also read your device's advertising ID where Android makes it available. OpenReader shows no ads and does not use this identifier for advertising; you can reset or delete it in Android's settings (Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Google > Ads, depending on your device).

We use this information only to find and fix crashes and to understand which features are used, so we can improve the app. It does not include the names, paths or contents of your documents, and we do not sell it or use it for advertising. Collection is always on in release builds; the app has no switch to turn it off. The identifiers above are reset when you clear the app's data or reinstall it.

Google processes this data on our behalf under its own terms. See Privacy and Security in Firebase and the Google Privacy Policy. Google keeps this data for the retention periods set for the project in Firebase and Google Analytics, after which it is deleted.

Update checks

On Android, the app asks Google Play whether a newer version is available. On Windows, macOS and Linux, it asks GitHub for the latest release of OpenReader, and downloads the installer only if you choose to update. These requests reveal your network address to Google or GitHub, as any connection does, but carry no information about your documents.

File and storage access

On Android, OpenReader asks for the "All files access" permission (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE). It uses it only to find supported documents (such as PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, CSV, text and Markdown files) anywhere on your device's storage and to open them, because your documents can be in any folder. You can decline it and still open files shared from other apps or picked with Open file, and you can grant or revoke it at any time in Android's settings (Settings > Permissions in the app shows how).

On Windows, macOS and Linux, OpenReader reads documents from the folders you browse and from files you open or drop onto it.

Files are read and rendered locally and are never sent anywhere. OpenReader changes a file only when you explicitly delete it from within the app, after confirming.

Sharing and links

When you share a document or the app, or tap Rate, OpenReader hands off to the system share sheet or to Google Play. What happens next is governed by the app you choose and its own privacy policy.

Children's privacy

OpenReader is a general-purpose app and is not directed at children under 13. It does not ask for names, email addresses or other contact details from anyone. The crash and usage data described above is collected from all users of the Android app.

Your choices and rights

You can stop all data collection by uninstalling the app, and reset its identifiers by clearing the app's data. Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask about, or ask us to delete, data collected about you; contact us below. Because the data is tied to random identifiers rather than to your name, we may need your app-instance identifier to find it.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published at this same page with a revised "Last updated" date.

Contact

Questions about this policy or the app can be raised via GitHub Issues. For privacy requests, such as asking about or deleting data collected about you, email c.dipu0@gmail.com.
